Category 1: Employer of Record (EOR)

An employer of record lets a company hire people in any country where it has no entity. Buyers search country by country, and the leaders answer with a page for every country, every service in it and every role hired there. Paybooks covers one country today. This is the plan to build the global footprint.

FINDINGS

Three findings decide the strategy.

Buyers search country by country.

“Employer of record [country]” searches add up to 11,180 a month across 26 countries, almost as much as the core “employer of record” searches (11,520). India adds 8,570. The country page is the product page.

Every global leader builds a page per country.

9 of 11 competitors publish pages for 113 to 186 countries. 4 go further, with 767 to 1,157 country guides each on payroll, benefits, termination and work permits. Skuad adds 338 role pages.

Paybooks starts from one country.

Paybooks has 14 Employer of Record pages, all about India, and they rank for one keyword. Outside India there is no page to fix, so the new site can be built right from day one.

03 · PAYBOOKS TODAY

One country, fourteen pages, one keyword.

Paybooks' Employer of Record content covers India only: 14 pages and about 34,561 words, ranking for one keyword. The faults below are fixed in the India country cluster.

1country with an EOR page today
14EOR pages, all about India
1keyword ranking, “eor solutions” at #8 in India
8 issues on the current pages, most damaging first
#IssueEvidenceFix in the plan
1High
The same article is published twice.
"Employer of Record in India: Fast & Compliant Expansion for Startups" and "Why Global Tech Startups Are Using Employer of Record India in 2026" are 90% identical, word for word. The second is the first republished with the year changed, and the first still says 2025 on the page. Both are indexed, and a third startup article from 2024 targets the same search.Merge all three into the EOR India page and redirect.
2High
Two service pages compete for the same search.
/eor/ and /eor-2/ are both indexed, both in the sitemap, and each names itself as the main version. /eor-2/ is a thin 479-word page titled "EOR Services at Best Price", with two main headings, one a leftover template line: "Find the Information You're Looking For".Redirect /eor-2/ to the new EOR India page and drop it from the sitemap.
3High
The EOR service page's headline is about payroll.
The main heading on /eor/ reads "Paybooks is the Payroll Solution Choice of 3,000+ Leading Companies". The page has no meta description and is marked up as a blog article, not a service. Its only ranking search is "paybooks login", at #23.The new page leads with Employer of Record India, carries a description and service markup.
4High
None of the 12 EOR articles has a main heading.
The article template shows each title but does not mark it as the page's main heading (H1). Google loses the clearest signal of what each page is about.Fix the article template once; the fix carries to every article that uses it.
5Medium
The page that sells hides the price.
/eor/ shows no price and no tables at 1,697 words. Skuad ranks with $199 and 19 tables on the page.Show $199 and every statutory cost line, with a calculator.
6Medium
The articles are buried.
Each EOR article has just 2 internal links pointing to it. /eor/ receives 265 but links to almost none of them, including the 5,210-word hiring guide.Hub and spoke: every guide links to the EOR page and the EOR page links back.
7Medium
Two definition articles compete.
"What is Employer of Record (EOR) Services?" from 2024 and the 2026 "EOR Guide: Costs, Risks and Use Cases" answer the same question.Merge into one short glossary answer and redirect.
8Medium
The dates signal stale content.
The only page that ranks carries 2025 in its title. Two 2024 articles have never been updated since they were published.Refresh to 2026 with a visible reviewed date.

Checked on the live pages, 24 September 2026. Rankings from Ahrefs.

04 · SITE ARCHITECTURE

One hub, a product page per country, and everything under it.

Six layers, each answering a different search. Links run down from the hub to every country, across between a country's pages, and back up to the hub.

1
Global EOR hub/employer-of-record/

The page for the head searches: what Paybooks does, how it works, who it fits, what it costs, and a country picker. Every other page links up to it.

Sample built: the landing page in this proposal.

2
Country product pages/employer-of-record/[country]/

One page per country Paybooks covers: hire there without an entity, the local contract, benefits and costs, timeline, and a quote form. This is the product page buyers compare.

Sample built: Employer of Record India, the depth every country page follows.

3
Services in each country/payroll/[country]/ · /contractors/[country]/

The other Paybooks services per country, cross-linked with the EOR page: payroll for companies with their own entity, and hiring or converting contractors.

Links Category 1 to Category 2 (Multi-Country Payroll) and, in India, to Category 3 (Managed India Office).

4
Country guides/employer-of-record/[country]/[topic]/

8 guides per country: hiring guide, employer cost, payroll and taxes, benefits, leave and holidays, termination and severance, work permits and visas, minimum wage and salaries. They answer the questions buyers ask before they hire.

The leaders run 767 to 1,157 of these pages each.

5
Role × country pages/hire/[role]/[country]/

“Hire a software developer in Poland.” One template, filled with each country's salary band, all-in cost and notice rules.

India alone carries 16,350 searches a month across 15 roles (India deep dive).

6
Tools, comparisons and glossary/tools/ · /compare/ · /glossary/

Employer cost calculator for every country, salary benchmarks, Paybooks vs each competitor, best EOR providers, EOR vs PEO, and a glossary built to be quoted by AI answers.

06 · PROGRAMMATIC RULES

Hundreds of pages, none of them thin.

Templates make the scale possible. These rules keep every page worth ranking.

Real data on every pageEach country page carries its own employer costs, contract rules, benefits, notice periods and payroll dates, taken from the country's official sources and dated. A page without them stays unpublished.
Only countries Paybooks servesA country goes live only when Paybooks can employ there. Every page states what Paybooks does in that country.
Launch in batchesWave 1 countries first. The next batch ships once the first is indexed and ranking.
Links in three directionsDown from the hub to each country, across between a country's pages, and up from every page to the hub and the quote form.
Built for AI answersA short answer at the top of each page, question headings, tables and FAQ markup, so answer engines can quote Paybooks.
Refreshed every yearRates, thresholds and holidays updated each year, with the review date shown on the page.
